# Chemical consumption history and review alerts

The private archive in the CRM's `ChemicalConsumptionHistory` database table was generated from the twelve monthly PDFs in the user-provided 2025.zip. Customer quantities and property identities are never committed to GitHub. It contains 3,303 reconciled PDF blocks aggregated into 332 historical property identities, 1,593 property/product/unit series and 16,012 monthly observations. All accepted block costs reconcile to their printed total within $0.05. Quantities retain their original units. Multiple locations sharing the same Skimmer code are aggregated at property level; they are not individual water-body readings.

Identity matching uses a unique Skimmer code, otherwise a unique normalized full property name. Ambiguous archive identities and many-to-one CRM matches are not assigned. Code-less historical names are not automatically merged with differently coded records. The API reports unmatched CRM properties; these need identity review, not fuzzy matching.

An absent chemical in a reconciled property report is provisionally treated as zero *recorded* consumption. An absent property report is missing data, never zero. This assumption and service/chemical concentration changes require operational review.

The offline exercise identified 156 statistical outlier observations. Outlier flags are retained with the original quantities. For regular series (at least nine observed and nine positive months), quantities were normalized by month length and the chemical's seasonal factor when available. The exclusion rule was median +/- 3.5 * 1.4826 * median absolute deviation (MAD). When MAD was zero, Q1 - 3 IQR and Q3 + 3 IQR were used; zero MAD and IQR did not trigger automatic exclusions. Intermittent series were not automatically trimmed.

Seasonal daily factors are available for liquid chlorine and muriatic acid. The cohort contains only full-year properties with positive consumption in at least nine months. Each daily quantity is divided by that property's median positive daily consumption; the median of these ratios is taken across properties for each month. Factors are normalized to weighted annual mean 1. At least ten eligible properties per month are required. A single year does not establish independently validated seasonality; shared factors are preliminary and are applied to each property's own baseline. Other regular products use only a days adjustment, explicitly labeled without a seasonal factor.

Automatic review bounds use the mean +/- 3 sample standard deviations of retained, daily, deseasonalized observations, clipped at zero, then multiplied by the target month's days and chemical factor. At least nine observed and nine positive months and six retained observations are required. This is a provisional review rule, not a confidence guarantee, dosage instruction, or water-quality threshold. An excess alert is raised only when the recorded quantity is greater than the upper bound. Excess quantity and percent are measured against the upper bound, not the mean. Means reported in the UI are cleaned observed monthly means; the expected target month is separately modeled.

Manual min/max references take priority and retain the existing manually configured period factor. Historical automatic bounds are never multiplied by that period factor again. Unmatched identities and short/intermittent histories receive no automatic classification. Budget alerts remain separate from quantity alerts.

For 2025 months the API can present matched archive consumption when there is no imported consumption for the CRM period. Existing fee data is retained; imported consumption takes priority. These archive views do not write to consumption periods. Editing or importing a period continues to use existing endpoints. The additive migration creates the private history table. Load the user-approved local JSON using `node scripts/import-consumption-history.cjs path/to/archive.json --apply` after deploying migrations and generating Prisma. The importer skips existing year/code entries; it never overwrites consumption, fees or existing history. Archive quantities used to fit the model are in-sample and are not independent validation.

The frontend overview lists quantity overages for all recorded properties in the selected month, with the product, unit, upper bound, excess quantity and percentage; choosing an alert opens that property. Empty months and unassessed products are explicitly distinguished from evaluated consumption without an overage.
